ZSA Moonlander

Brand Identity & Campaign For A High-Tech Keyboard

student project

The existing visual language relied heavily on skeuomorphic elements and lacked a clear contemporary identity.

This project builds a simplified brand identity and a campaign concept that reflects the keyboard’s modular design philosophy and technical character.

Brand Direction

The brand direction focuses on clarity and modularity, reflecting the engineering mindset of programmable keyboard users.

A simplified logomark replaces the previous skeuomorphic logo. Its double outline follows the two-layer keycaps and the split layout of the boards, and it stays clear at small sizes.

The wordmark is set in a serif, Corundum Text Bold, which reads as something built to last rather than a tech startup.

Identity System

The guidelines set out the lockups and their minimum sizes, the logo on different backgrounds and as a social media avatar, and the misuses to avoid. Deep navy is the base colour, with mid purple as a small accent that never competes with it. DM Serif Display and Inter carry communication and stay separate from the logo type.

Applications

The identity on a trade-show booth and on merchandise: a USB-C adapter, a USB stick and badges.

Campaign

Through bold compositions and close-up product visuals, the design emphasizes precision, customization, and the tactile experience of mechanical keyboards.

The visual system extends across multiple campaign touchpoints including billboards, editorials, and social media graphics, maintaining a consistent visual language built around precision and modular structure.

The campaign images were made for a photography course and carry the keyboard’s own icon, not the new Z mark. I shot the keyboard myself in the studio, every frame at f/11, 1/160 s and ISO 80.

Sketches

The Z started as pencil sketches on grid paper. Three digital directions followed, then tests of the wordmark in sans and serif type and in muted blue, grey and teal.
